she made me write again

✦ · ✦

I didn’t think I’d find my way back to writing. For a while, it felt like the words had left me for good, and I stopped even trying to call them back. But then she appeared, in the gentlest way, and suddenly there were sentences forming in my head again. Not perfect ones, not polished, but real. It’s strange how someone can bring that out of you without meaning to, just by being who they are.

She doesn’t know it, but the way she makes me feel has its own language. I catch myself reaching for words to capture it, afraid that if I don’t, the feeling will slip away. I used to think my writing was tired, that it had run out of use, but with her, it feels like a beginning instead of an ending. Like something fragile waking up after being asleep for too long.

It’s not about grand gestures or inspiration that burns bright and then fades. It’s quieter than that. Just the small ways she makes me feel understood, or the way her presence lingers in my thoughts long after we’ve stopped talking. Those moments don’t demand to be written—they ask softly, and I find myself answering.

What surprises me is how natural it feels again. The words don’t weigh as much as they used to; they come easier, lighter, as if they’re glad to be let out. And when I write now, I don’t do it just to ease the heaviness in me. I write because she gave me something worth holding onto, something worth putting down in words.

She made me want to write again, and that in itself feels almost miraculous. It’s not often that someone can breathe new life into the parts of you you thought were gone for good. There’s a rare kind of beauty in it, a quiet revival that leaves you both shaken and grateful. Now, every word feels alive in my hands, every sentence carrying a pulse I’d forgotten it could have. And every time I finish a page, I realize—I’m not just writing for me anymore. I’m writing because she exists.

— J.
